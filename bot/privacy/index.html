Do Not Research

Privacy Policy

For the Do Not Research Discord bot and the members portal it works with. In effect from 17 November 2026.

In short

  • The bot is run by Do Not Research in Do Not Research's own Discord server. It can read what is posted in the channels it has been let into, and the list of people in the server.
  • Do Not Research keeps this to run the organization: memberships and roles, classes and events, its archive and history, and to understand how the community is doing.
  • Do Not Research matches Discord accounts to its own records of members, students and contributors.
  • None of it is sold, used for advertising, or used to train AI models. You can ask to see, correct or delete what is held about you.

Who runs it

Do Not Research Incorporated, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization. Questions and requests go to info@donotresearch.org.

What the bot does

  1. Roles. It gives and takes back the roles that follow a membership (Member, Supporter, Sustainer, Facilitator), a class (for example Bootcamp) and its end (Alumni), and published work (Sleeper). It only removes a role that it gave.
  2. Recognition. It counts what people do in the server so that members can earn patches on their page in the members portal.
  3. Events. It reads the server's events and who is interested in them, can add Do Not Research's events to the server, and notes who was present in a voice or stage room during a session. It never records sound or video.
  4. Keeping records in step. It reads the server's rules, announcements and event posts so that Do Not Research's website and records say the same thing.
  5. The archive. Do Not Research keeps a copy of what is posted in the server's channels as part of its own history, which it has published in books and on its website since 2021.
  6. Knowing its members. It reads the list of people in the server, with their roles and the date they joined, and Do Not Research matches accounts to its records (see "Matching").
  7. Reports for the officers. Do Not Research counts and studies activity in the server to plan its programs and report on its work.
  8. Safety. Officers and moderators may use what the bot holds to enforce the server's rules.
  9. Messages from the bot. It may post in the server, and may send a direct message about a person's membership, role, class or event.

What is collected

About each account in the server: the Discord user ID, username, display name and avatar; the roles it holds; the date it joined; and whether it is still in the server.

About what is posted in channels the bot can see, including members' channels and their history back to 2020: the text of each message, its attachments, links and embeds, who posted it, where and when, the reactions it received and who gave them, and threads with their titles.

About sessions: who was in a voice or stage room, and when. About events: who marked themselves interested.

When a member links their account on the members portal, Discord asks their permission to share account details. The portal keeps the Discord ID and names, the membership account it is linked to, the email address verified at sign-in by ITM, the date of the link, and a record of each role the bot gave or removed. If the portal ever asks for more at that step (for example the email address on the Discord account, or the accounts connected to it), it will say so on Discord's own permission screen before anything is shared.

Not collected: private messages between members; anything in a channel the bot has not been let into; sound or video; passwords; payment details, which are held by ITM and its payment processor.

Matching

Do Not Research joins a Discord account to a person in its own records: a membership, a class, published work, or a name on its contributor list. A match can come from the person (by linking their account, or by telling Do Not Research their Discord name on a form) or from Do Not Research (from what the account shows in the server, from public profiles, and from what officers know). A match made without the person is checked by an officer. If you think a match is wrong, or do not want one, write to info@donotresearch.org.

What it is used for

To give and remove access to members' spaces; to award patches; to run classes and events and know who came; to keep the website, the events record and the contributor list right; to keep Do Not Research's history; to reach members and contributors about their membership, their class, their published work or their payment; to plan programs and report on the organization's work, including to funders, in totals; and to enforce the server's rules.

It is not sold, licensed or used for advertising. It is not used to train AI models.

Who sees it

The officers of Do Not Research and people working at their direction. It is not shared with anyone else, except the services that hold or handle it for Do Not Research: Discord; ITM (itm.studio), which holds memberships, payments and sign-in; Render, which hosts the members portal and its database in the United States; GitHub and Google, where Do Not Research keeps its private records; and Anthropic, whose AI assistant the officers use to organize those records. Each has its own privacy policy. Reports that leave Do Not Research give totals, not people.

How long it is kept

For as long as Do Not Research runs the server and keeps its archive, unless you ask for it to be deleted. The record of role changes is kept so that the bot never removes a role it did not give. Deleting a post on Discord does not delete a copy Do Not Research already holds; ask for that as below.

Your choices

Write to info@donotresearch.org, from the email on your membership or with your Discord username, to: see what is held about you; have it corrected; have it deleted; unlink your Discord account; or object to being matched or counted. A person handles requests, normally within 30 days. Leaving the server stops anything new being collected about you; it does not delete what is already held, so ask.

If you are in the European Union or the United Kingdom: Do Not Research relies on its legitimate interest in running a membership community and keeping its history, your data is stored in the United States, and you have the rights above together with the right to complain to your data protection authority.

Age

Discord requires its users to be at least 13, or older where local law says so. The bot is not meant for anyone younger, and Do Not Research does not knowingly keep information about children under 13.
